Deep Residual Learning of a Probabilistic’ Partial
Least Squares Model for Predictive Data Analytics
Zhiqiang Ge
, Senior Member, IEEE, Duxin Chen
, Member, IEEE, and Wenwu Yu
, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Recently,
probabilistic
latent
variable
models
have played an important role in data analytics in various
industrial application scenarios, such as process monitoring, fault
diagnosis, and soft sensing. Inspired by the idea of lightweight
deep learning, this article proposes a new deep residual learning
method for the probabilistic’ partial least squares (PLSs) model.
First, layerwise probabilistic modeling is carried out to extract
supervised latent variables in different hidden layers of the deep
model using a well-designed expectation-maximization algorithm
for parameter optimization. Through this layerwise residual
learning process, more target-related latent variables can be
extracted, which are supervised by the outputs of the predictive
model. Next, an additional probabilistic model is constructed
for information fusion and further extraction of supervised
latent variables which are highly related to the modeling target.
In fact, this step can be considered as an ensemble learning
strategy, which has great potentials in decreasing modeling
error
and
reducing
prediction
uncertainty.
A
soft-sensing
strategy is then developed for online prediction of key variables.
The performance is evaluated using two industrial examples.
Compared to the shallow probabilistic model, the performance
of the deep model has been improved by 10%–20%.
Impact Statement—PLSs model has been widely used for
predictive data analytics. While a lightweight deep learning
form of the basic model has recently been developed, it still
lacks probabilistic interpretations for predictive data modeling
and analysis. Inspired by the ideas of both deep learning and
residual learning, a new deep probabilistic learning form of
the PLSs model is proposed in this article. Through a layer-
by-layer residual learning process, more target-related latent
variables can be extracted, upon which an additional proba-
bilistic model is constructed for further information fusion and
ensemble learning. Based on detailed comparative case studies,
both prediction accuracy and uncertainty have been improved
by the deep probabilistic model.
Index Terms—Deep learning, industrial data analytics, partial
least squares (PLSs), predictive modeling, probabilistic latent
variable model, residual modeling.

I. INTRODUCTION
I
N the past ten years, data analytics have played an important
role in Industry 4.0 by providing valuable information mined
from industrial big data [1], [2], [3]. Typical application sce-
narios for industrial data analytics include process monitoring,
soft sensing of key parameters, fault diagnosis, machine health
management, remaining useful life prediction, key performance
indicator prediction, and optimization [4], [5], [6], [7], [8].
To support these application scenarios, various data-driven
modeling approaches need to be constructed of which pre-
dictive modeling is one of the most popular methods. For
example, to provide online estimation of some difﬁcult-to-
measure variables for quality control, an effective predic-
tive model is required to make real-time predictions from
other easy-to-measure process variables. A similar predictive
model is also desired for remaining useful life prediction of
a key equipment in the industrial processes, to ensure the
implementation of prognostic health management [9], [10],
[11], [12].
For the purpose of industrial data predictive modeling, many
statistical and machine learning methods have been introduced
and used in the past decades. As well, deep learning has recently
become popular [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18]. According
to the search results from both Google Scholar and Web of
Science, although more complicated models (shallow and deep)
have been introduced, the linear regression models still play a
dominant role in predictive modeling of practical industrial data
analytics, such as least square regression, principal component
regression, and partial least squares (PLSs) regression. Due to
their simple and transparent model structures, they are more
likely to provide reliable, explainable, and trustworthy results.
These are the main reasons why the simple linear models are
still very popular in the current ages of deep learning and
artiﬁcial intelligence.
With more sensors installed in modern industrial processes,
the number of measured variables has grown rapidly, which
makes data mining and analytics more complicated. To alle-
viate this problem, latent variable models use a good balance
between dimensionality reduction and modeling performance
for predictive data analytics [19]. Principal component analysis
(PCA) and PLSs are two common methods that have been
widely studied in the past years. By projecting the original high-
dimensional process variables onto a low-dimensional feature
space, both analytics and regression modeling become much
easier and more efﬁcient. By considering the stochastic nature
of those measured variables in practical industrial processes,

another type of latent variable model may be ﬁt for predic-
tive analytics, which is known as a probabilistic latent vari-
able model. Some representatives include probabilistic PCA,
probabilistic PCR, and probabilistic PLS [20], [21], [22], [23],
[24], [25]. Compared to the basic PCA and PLS models that
are typically developed through data projection and coordinate
transformation, probabilistic latent variable models are mostly
built upon generative model structures. By assuming prior prob-
abilities for latent variables, continuous inferences of their pos-
terior probabilities can be implemented given the observed data
based on which, the optimized probabilistic model structure
can be determined. As a result, the probabilistic latent variable
model can naturally deal with missing data in the model training
process. Model selection can also be incorporated into the learn-
ing algorithm. As well, the generative form of the probabilistic
latent variable model is more ﬂexible and straightforward for its
extension to more complex models, to obtain higher capacities
and prediction performances.
The idea of deep learning has been considered as the most
effective way to increase the complexity of a data-driven model.
At the same time, however, several drawbacks also arise such
as high computational burden, cumbersome parameter tuning,
and weak model interpretability, which creates obstacles in the
practical industrial application of these deep learning methods.
Recently, several lightweight forms of deep learning models
have been developed for industrial data analytics, i.e., deep
PCA and deep PLS, which are actually combinations of deep
learning and latent variable models [26], [27], [28], [29], [30],
[31], [32]. Due to the lightweight model structure, fewer tuning
parameters, and a relatively smaller training dataset, those deep
latent-variable models seem quite suitable and useful in various
industrial application scenarios for data analytics.
Inspired by the idea of lightweight deep learning imple-
mented using the PLS model [32], this article extends the
probabilistic PLS model to its deep learning form. A deep
residual learning algorithm is proposed based on the generative
structure of the probabilistic PLS model, with a well-designed
expectation-maximization learning strategy for parameter opti-
mization. Furthermore, a soft sensor is constructed based on the
newly developed deep probabilistic PLS model for online pre-
diction of key performance indicators in industrial processes.
II. A BRIEF INTRODUCTION TO THE PROBABILISTIC
PLS MODEL
The basic idea of a probabilistic PLS model is to use one
part of the extracted latent variables to build the relationship
between input and output variables, while another part of the
latent variable is only used to explain the input variables. Based
on the generative modeling framework, the probabilistic PLS
model is as follows [20]:
x = μx + Pts + Qtu + ex
y = μy + Cts + ey
(1)
where μx and μy are the mean values of the input variables x
and the output variables y, P ∈Rm×qs, C ∈Rr×qs and Q ∈
Rm×qb are loading matrices, ts ∈Rqs×1 is the supervised latent
variable vector that is used to connect the information of x and
y, tu ∈Rqu×1 is the unsupervised latent variable vector that
is only used to explain the information of x, ex ∈Rm×1 and
ey ∈Rr×1 are the measurement noises of x and y.
In this model, it is assumed that both latent variables
and measurement noises are normally distributed. Thus, the
prior probabilities for supervised and unsupervised latent
variables are given as p(ts) = N(0, I), p(tu) = N(0, I),
and
the
probabilities
for
the
two
measurement
noises
are
p(ex) = N(0, Σx),
and
p(ey) = N(0, Σy),
where
Σx = diag{σ2
x,i}i=1,2,··· ,m,
Σy = diag{σ2
y,j}j=1,2,··· ,r.
It
can be noted that different noise variances have been assumed
for the different process variables.

B. Cement Clinker Production Process
Cement is an important building material. Its production pro-
cess contains several operating units, including the preheater,
the calciner, the rotary kiln, and the grate cooler. A ﬂowchart
of this process is shown in Fig. 6, which is from a real cement
company in East China. In cement production, the content of
the free calcium oxide (f-CaO) in the clinker is a critical fac-
tor, which signiﬁcantly affects the quality of the ﬁnal product.
Therefore, a quality control system is required to monitor this
variable. In practice, however, there is currently no effective
instrument that can provide continuous measurements for this
important variable. As a result, the value of f-CaO content in
the clinker has to be measured ofﬂine through manual sampling
and laboratory analysis, which are both time consuming and
expensive. Therefore, in this case, a data-driven soft sensor is
particularly useful, which can provide real-time estimations for
the content of f-CaO in the clinker.
To predict the f-CaO content in the cement clinker, all of
the 103 routinely recorded process variables are used as the
input for the soft sensor. For model training and performance
evaluation, the complete dataset which contains 600 effective
data samples is partitioned into two equal parts. To develop the
deep probabilistic PLS model, the number of hidden layers in
this case study is selected as 5, while the numbers of supervised
and unsupervised latent variables are selected as 10 and 5 in
both the basic and layerwise probabilistic PLS models. In the
ﬁnal probabilistic PLS model, the numbers of supervised and
unsupervised latent variables are selected as 8 and 4, respec-
tively. Similar to the ﬁrst case study, those parameters were also
determined using the cross-validation method.
Detailed prediction results of the f-CaO content for the 300
testing data samples by the shallow and deep probabilistic
model-based soft sensors are shown in Fig. 7. As can be seen
from Fig. 7(a), the basic PPLS model has some very bad pre-
diction points which are negative that are highlighted by two
ellipses. Again, by comparing the values of the RMSE index,
the soft sensing performance has been improved by 10% using
the deep probabilistic model, while the value of the R2 index has
increased by more than 15%. The parity plot for both models
is shown in Fig. 8.
Similarly, the soft sensing performances of different layer-
wise probabilistic PLS models are compared to the ﬁnal prob-
abilistic PLS model in Table VI. As can be seen, although the
ﬁnal probabilistic PLS model has obtained the best prediction
result, different layerwise models have shown quite different
soft sensing performances. Therefore, again, the quality of the
supervised latent variables in each hidden layer of the deep
model is difﬁcult to evaluate and predict. First, the probabilistic
model itself has some uncertainties in the process of parameter
learning. Second, the number of supervised latent variables
selected for each hidden layer is a trial-and-error process, which
may introduce additional uncertainty for soft sensing. As well,
the deep learning model structure has its own layer-by-layer
uncertainty in feature extraction.

V. CONCLUSION
In this article, a deep residual form of the probabilistic PLS
model has been proposed, which was inspired by the main
idea of the recently developed lightweight deep latent variable
model. Using deep learning on the simple latent variable model,
both effectiveness and modeling efﬁciency can be obtained.
Based on layerwise probabilistic modeling and extraction of su-
pervised latent variables, an additional probabilistic PLS model
has been constructed for information fusion and reextraction of
supervised latent variables, which has the potential to reduce
prediction uncertainty and thus keep the deep model more stable
in practical applications. Two industrial examples have been
provided for case studies of soft sensor application based on
the developed method. Compared to the shallow probabilistic
model, the performance of the deep model has been improved
by 10%–20%.

there are several critical issues that deserve further study. First,
